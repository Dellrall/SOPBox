# SOPBox impact: the numbers for the presentation

The figures below are the ones worth putting on a slide: each comes from a named, checkable source, and most are
Malaysian. Use the formulas with an outlet's own numbers; until a pilot gives real ones, show the low and high case
side by side so the claim is never overstated.

## 1. The problem in Malaysia (SDG 12.3)

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Food waste thrown away | **~17,000 tonnes a day** | SWCorp, 2021 data |
| Edible food thrown away | **~6,000 tonnes a day** (2025) | SWCorp, via NST, June 2026 |
| Food's share of the waste stream | **44.5%** | Bernama |
| Food waste's share of landfill methane | **~47%** | Malaysian waste-to-energy study (HRMARS) |
| Landfills full | projected by **2041** | SWCorp, via ISIS Malaysia, June 2026 |
| F&B outlets in Malaysia | **136,453**, gross output **RM99.0 billion** (2022) | DOSM Economic Census 2023 |

National policy: SWCorp and KPKT are preparing a food waste plan to 2030, and KPDN a National Food Bank Action Plan
2026–2030, both tied to SDG 12.3 (halve food waste by 2030).

## 2. The evidence that measuring cuts waste

**Malaysian kitchens: Papargyropoulou et al. (2019), *Sustainability* 11(21):6016**, five Malaysian hospitality and
food service case studies:

- food waste cost **23% of the value of food purchased**
- nearly **a third of food was wasted**, and **almost half of that was avoidable**
- the largest share was **preparation waste**; the causes were the kitchens' **operating procedures and policies**

The cases lean to hotels and buffets: present 23% as the high case, not the typical café.

**What measuring achieves: Champions 12.3 / WRI (2019)**, 114 restaurants in 12 countries:

- food waste down **26% on average in the first year**
- **RM7 saved for every RM1 invested** (reported as $7 : $1); the investment was **0.4% of food sales**
- **76% recovered the cost within one year**

SOPBox gives a kitchen that measurement without extra work: every count is compared with the stock it should have,
and the gap (waste, theft or a receiving mistake) is shown per storage area.

## 3. Operations in Malaysia (SDG 8)

| Fact | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurants short of workers | Presma and Primas (10,000+ restaurants) report **40–50% shortages**; Presma asked for **15,000 foreign workers** in October 2025 | The Edge; FMT |
| SMEs squeezed by costs | **57%** rising operating costs, **52%** labour costs; **45%** go digital mainly for productivity | Visa SME survey 2024 (800 SMEs) |
| Minimum wage | **RM1,700 a month ≈ RM8.17 an hour** | Minimum Wages Order 2024 |
| Manual entry errors | about **1–4% of typed fields** are wrong (2.8–3.7% in clinical studies) | peer-reviewed data-entry studies |

With staff this scarce, every hour not spent typing delivery notes or reconciling stock goes back to the kitchen.

## 4. Formulas

```
Waste cost (RM/month)     = food purchases per month × waste share     # 23% high case; 10% café case; own count gap once known
Year-one saving (RM/month)= waste cost × 26%                           # Champions 12.3
Hours freed (h/month)     = deliveries per month × (minutes typing − minutes with SOPBox) / 60
Labour value (RM/month)   = hours freed × RM8.17
Food cost % (actual)      = (opening stock + purchases − closing stock) / revenue × 100
```

**Worked example**, a café buying RM20,000 of food a month:

| Case | Waste cost | Year-one saving |
|---|---|---|
| Café case (10%) | RM2,000 a month | **≈ RM520 a month, RM6,240 a year** |
| High case (23%) | RM4,600 a month | **≈ RM1,196 a month, RM14,352 a year** |

## 5. What will convince judges most

Our own pilot numbers. With 2–3 outlets:

1. Time 20 delivery notes typed by hand against 20 read by SOPBox, and count the mistakes in each.
2. Compare 2–4 weeks of count gaps (RM) before and after using SOPBox.

Put those beside the benchmarks above.
